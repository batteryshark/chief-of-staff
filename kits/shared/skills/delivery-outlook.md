# Delivery outlook

Give the principal a useful owner judgment, not manufactured certainty. Read the
agreed finish and quality bar, current owner record, accepted results and live holds.
Preserve accepted work, required tests and risk-proportionate verification.

## Build the outlook

1. Name the agreed user-visible finish and quality. Separate what works now from
   remaining outcomes; use a few outcomes, not a task inventory. Identify the critical
   path, dependencies and principal-only hands; say what continues independently.
2. Date and attribute the OWNER's provisional/conditional SWAG: a useful range or
   best guess, confidence and major assumptions. A percentage is optional, not a
   requirement. Do not refuse all estimation because evidence is imperfect, force a
   date, or turn a forecast into a commitment. If finish genuinely cannot be ranged,
   forecast the next demonstrable milestone/evidence and give the owner's judgment
   about what that will settle.
3. State actual staffing and supported remaining model/resource cost, or UNKNOWN.
   Distinguish measured facts from allowances; do not infer cost from word counts,
   model anecdotes or invented throughput. Respect current capacity, model-named
   exceptions and paid-use permissions; this skill grants none.
4. Retain the previous forecast as “was”, with the revision cause. Reuse the CURRENT
    owner record rather than stale executive summaries. The chief may critique it,
    attributed as chief judgment, never laundering that critique into an owner commitment.
5. Offer useful choices: real finish change, authorized resource/approach change,
   explicit principal scope/quality tradeoff, or park. Explain effects and recommend
   one. No automatic cuts, parks, quality relaxation or hold clearance. Covered
   technical choices stay owner-owned; only material reserved tradeoffs need principal
   iteration. End with the next owner-owned outcome and any exact decision needed.

## Revisit on evidence, not ritual

Material variance or scope growth triggers early owner judgment before committing
to an expensive detour. Two checkpoints without visible output are a DIAGNOSTIC CUE,
not automatic failure, a timebox or a park rule: uncertainty reduction can be progress.
Ask what changed, whether this is still the simplest route, and what useful evidence
comes next. Preserve actual safety/authorization holds and required tests.

Keep the current outlook in the existing owner log/report and link it from the
short status Note; do not create a parallel dashboard or forecasting service.
Use the event bus for a real answer or material intervention; if unavailable, preserve
the delivery gap in the owner record and use the current chief's authorized route.
Retain original session, absolute directory, private
reference and scope. A processing receipt is not approval or owner completion.
